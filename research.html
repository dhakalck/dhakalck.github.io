Research Agenda

What works, for whom, at what cost?

My research program combines methodological rigor with policy relevance across three interconnected themes. Whether evaluating a U.S. safety-net program or building a cost-effectiveness model for a ministry of health, the goal is the same: credible evidence that decision-makers can act on.

Theme 1

Safety-Net Programs & Economic Shocks

How do economic policies and social programs affect vulnerable populations — particularly food security, health outcomes, and financial well-being during economic disruptions? Using large-scale household and scanner data with quasi-experimental designs, this work has examined:

Unemployment insurance and food hardship. Analyzing the expiration of the $600/week Federal Pandemic Unemployment Compensation across 18+ million households, I estimated a 9.7% reduction in food spending and a 6.0% decline in food sufficiency, using a control-function approach within an endogenous switching regression framework (Journal of Policy Analysis and Management, 2024).

SNAP and household food purchasing. Using Nielsen Homescan data with billions of transaction records, I estimated demand elasticities with the Exact Affine Stone Index demand system to quantify SNAP’s impact on nutritional quality and spending patterns (Nutrients, 2021).

Ongoing: working papers on the 2020 Economic Impact Payments, the 2021 Child Tax Credit expansion and expiration, and the SNAP emergency allotment cliff — see Publications.

Theme 2

Public Health Interventions & Behavioral Economics

Can incentive design move population health outcomes? This theme evaluates innovative public health interventions through behavioral economics frameworks and rigorous causal inference:

State vaccine lottery programs. As co-first author, I evaluated lottery-based COVID-19 vaccine incentives across multiple states using 403,714 individual responses and state-level vaccination data, applying difference-in-differences with augmented synthetic control methods. We found a 2.1% overall uptake increase with substantial heterogeneity — the programs were ineffective in several states — a finding with direct implications for incentive design (JAMA Network Open, 2021).

Risky health behaviors during COVID-19. Evidence from expenditures on alcohol, non-alcoholic beverages, and tobacco products documented how the pandemic shifted consumption patterns (PLOS ONE, 2022, co-first author), alongside work on eviction risk and adult mental health (Preventive Medicine Reports, 2022).

Theme 3

Chronic Disease Epidemiology & Machine Learning

Leveraging machine learning and advanced statistical methods to predict disease risk, identify modifiable risk factors, and inform screening:

Diabetes prediction. Developed and validated machine learning models (Random Forest, XGBoost, neural networks) for Type 2 diabetes prediction using BRFSS data, achieving 85%+ accuracy in identifying high-risk individuals (Int. J. of Environmental Research and Public Health, 2021, co-first author).

Trends and disparities. A decade-long analysis (2012–2022) of diabetes prevalence documented widening disparities by race/ethnicity, education, and region (American Journal of Preventive Medicine, 2024), extended to regional disparities in risk factors (Diabetes, Obesity & Metabolism, 2024) and the heterogeneous link between e-cigarette use and diabetes (AJPM Focus, 2025).

In progress: a revise-and-resubmit on rising coronary heart disease prevalence in the U.S., 2015–2022.

Cross-Cutting

Beyond the three themes

Global Health Economics

Economic Evaluation for Priority-Setting

Cost-effectiveness and budget-impact analyses for NCDs, cancer screening, maternal health, and road safety across 19 LMICs — plus decision-modeling training for the next generation of health economists.

Climate & Agriculture

Adaptation Economics

Climate change adaptation in Nepal: a 21.6% revenue increase and 6.4% risk reduction from adaptation strategies (Resources, Environment and Sustainability), alongside work on organic manure productivity and crop yield response.

AI & NLP

Language Models for Food & Health

AgriBERT transformer models for agricultural text analysis (IJCAI 2022), large language models for food applications (IEEE Transactions on Big Data), and research on human-like biases in LLMs.
