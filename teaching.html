Teaching

Turning students into analysts

Students learn economics best when every concept answers a question a manager, analyst, or policymaker actually asks: is this incentive worth its cost? Which patients benefit most? What will this policy do? My teaching turns students into analysts who can answer those questions with data.

Philosophy

Four principles

1

Active Learning Through Real Data

Students work with authentic datasets to answer substantive questions — not textbook exercises. As instructor of record at the University of Georgia, I taught econometric methods and computation in Stata and R using large-scale scanner data and the Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System — the same data behind my published research. Students cleaned large files, estimated models, and presented findings as decision memos.

2

Inclusive Excellence

Universal design principles supporting students from diverse backgrounds — extensive scaffolding paired with high expectations. As a TA for Futures and Options Markets and Principles of Microeconomics, I learned to ground abstract theory in concrete market examples before building to formal methods.

3

Methodological Transparency

Sharing failed analyses and research challenges to demystify the research process. Students see that credible research is iterative — and learn to interrogate their own results with the same skepticism they apply to others’.

4

Communication to Decision-Makers

Training students to translate technical analysis into accessible policy briefs and memos. The analysis is only half the job; the other half is making sure a busy decision-maker understands what it means.
