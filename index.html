Chandra Dhakal, PhD

I am a Health Economist at the CDC Foundation, supporting the Division of Global Health Protection at the CDC. I lead the Data to Policy (D2P) program under the Bloomberg Philanthropies Data for Health Initiative, working with ministries of health across 19 countries to turn rigorous evidence into better health policy.

My research sits at the intersection of health economics, causal inference, and economic evaluation — from safety-net programs and vaccine incentives in the United States to cost-effectiveness analysis for priority-setting in low- and middle-income countries.

About

From Nepal to global health policy

Originally from Nepal, my path has run through Kathmandu, Texas, and Georgia — from applied economics to the intersection of health economics, statistics, and global health policy. With a PhD in Applied Economics and an MS in Statistics from the University of Georgia, I specialize in causal inference, structural econometric modeling, and health economic evaluation.

I believe data is only as valuable as the policy it informs. My work aims to make complex simulation models and econometric analyses accessible to government leaders — supporting evidence-based resource allocation that prioritizes equity and maximizes population health.

Publications

Co-First Author

Lottery-Based Incentives and COVID-19 Vaccination Rates

JAMA Network Open · 2021

Evaluated state vaccine lottery programs using 400,000+ survey responses and synthetic control methods; found a 2.1% uptake increase with substantial state-level heterogeneity.

Unemployment Insurance Benefit Reduction and Food Hardship

Journal of Policy Analysis and Management · 2024

Estimated the effect of the $600/week Federal Pandemic Unemployment Compensation expiration on food spending and food sufficiency among 18+ million households.

Co-First Author

Machine Learning Prediction of Type 2 Diabetes

Int. J. of Environmental Research and Public Health · 2021

Developed and validated Random Forest, XGBoost, and neural network models for diabetes prediction using BRFSS data, achieving 85%+ accuracy.

Trends and Disparities in Diabetes Prevalence, 2012–2022

American Journal of Preventive Medicine · 2024

A decade-long analysis of diabetes prevalence trends across demographic subgroups, documenting widening disparities by race, education, and region.
